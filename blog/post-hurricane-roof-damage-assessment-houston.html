Post-Hurricane Roof Damage Assessment in Houston

By Josh, Lead Inspector • Published: August 19, 2026 • 10 Min Read • Storm Repair

You step out into your driveway after the feeder bands of a Gulf hurricane finally clear, and your stomach drops as you spot black shingle tabs scattered across your wet lawn. Water is already seeping through the drywall above your hallway ceiling, and you need to know right now how severe the structural damage is before mold takes over.

I have walked hundreds of Houston roofs across Katy, Pasadena, and Spring right after hurricane-force winds swept through. The hours immediately following a storm strike are chaotic, stressful, and overwhelming. Property owners frequently make two major mistakes: they climb onto a slick, compromised roof and risk falling through spongy plywood, or they wait days to inspect their attic, giving trapped rainwater time to rot their decking.

Here is your direct, ground-level assessment protocol to inspect your property safely, document windstorm damage for your carrier, and protect your home from severe secondary water ruin.

Step 1: Ground-Level Perimeter Inspection

Keep your boots firmly planted on solid ground. After a severe wind event in Southeast Texas, electrical lines may be tangled in tree limbs and roof decking can be structurally soft. Grab your smartphone and a pair of binoculars to perform a slow 360-degree perimeter walk around your property line.

  • Fallen Shingle Fragments: Walk your lawn, flower beds, and driveway. If you find architectural shingle sections or loose fiberglass tabs, note which side of the home they came from. That identifies which roof slope took the heaviest wind shear.
  • Torn Hip and Ridge Caps: Look straight up at the highest peaks of your roof line. Ridge caps take the direct brunt of 80 to 110 mph wind gusts and are frequently the first component stripped away, exposing the top decking gap.
  • Dented or Detached Gutters: Check your metal gutters. If fallen branches or high winds tore gutter runs away from the fascia board, rainwater will dump straight against your foundation and exterior walls.
  • Damaged Pipe Jacks and Vents: Examine the plumbing vent stacks and metal turtle vents on your back slope. High winds frequently twist metal exhaust hoods or peel rubber pipe collars back from PVC plumbing stacks.

How do I check my Houston roof for damage after a hurricane without climbing on it?

Stand in your yard with binoculars to check the ridge line for torn shingles, examine the perimeter ground for fallen roofing granules, and inspect metal downspouts for wind dents. Look for displaced pipe jacks and lifted flashing along exterior walls. In Texas windstorm zones, safety comes first: never step onto a wet, storm-damaged roof with compromised decking.

Step 2: Attic Inspection for Hidden Water Intrusion

The exterior roof surface is only half the story. The true extent of hurricane water intrusion shows up inside your attic. Put on sturdy work boots, grab a bright flashlight, and climb up through your ceiling access hatch.

Step only on solid wooden ceiling joists, never on bare sheetrock. Shine your light upward along the underside of the OSB or plywood roof decking:

  • Dark Water Stains and Dripping Sheathing: Check decking seams, especially around roof valleys and chimney saddles where wind-driven rain penetrates lifted flashing joints.
  • Wet Drops on Exposed Nail Shanks: If wind-driven rain forced water past the shingles and saturated the synthetic underlayment, you will see moisture clinging to the tips of roofing nails protruding through the wood.
  • Soggy Insulation: Saturated fiberglass or cellulose insulation clumps together and stops insulating. Squeeze a small sample; if water drips out, water entered directly above that ceiling area.
  • Daylight Beams: If you see rays of sunlight slicing through the wood sheathing, shingles and underlayment were stripped completely away by wind uplift.

When you spot an active leak, your immediate task is preventing ceiling drywall collapse. If you notice a sagging water pocket on your ceiling, poke the center with a screwdriver to channel trapped water into a bucket. This relieves heavy weight and stops widespread ceiling cave-ins.

Step 3: Understanding Shingle Wind Damage and Creasing

In Houston, standard architectural asphalt shingles carry a realistic lifespan of 15 to 20 years, while premium GAF systems reach 20 to 25 years maximum. However, when hurricane-force winds buffet an asphalt roof, the thermal adhesive seal strip snaps loose.

As wind lifts the unsealed tab upward, it bends the fiberglass backing mat, creating a sharp horizontal crease line across the shingle top where ceramic granules break off. You might not spot missing shingles from the street, but once that seal strip breaks, the roof has zero wind resistance left. The next thunderstorm will rip those creased shingles clean off your decking.

If rainfall is actively entering your living space through exposed wood sections, securing temporary emergency roof tarping within the first 24 hours stops soaked sheetrock and wood rot while fulfilling your policy requirement to protect the property from ongoing damage.

Step 4: The 30% Damage Threshold for Texas Insurance Claims

Homeowners frequently ask me whether their insurance company will approve a simple patch or buy a complete roof replacement. Under standard Texas windstorm guidelines, if an adjuster finds storm damage covering more than 30% of the roof surface across adjoining slopes, the carrier approves a full roof replacement because piecemeal patches void the manufacturer wind warranty of the home.

For a typical 2,000 square foot single-family home in Harris or Montgomery County, a full architectural shingle replacement runs between $9,500 and $16,000. When you carry standard RCV (Replacement Cost Value) coverage, most homeowners pay only their insurance deductible while the insurance carrier covers the remaining construction balance.

Before you file, ensure your damage is verified storm impact rather than routine age wear. When filing an urgent storm claim, providing date-stamped photos of lifted shingles, interior water spots, and damaged roof jacks speeds up your adjuster review and eliminates repair disputes.

Step 5: Immediate Next Steps Checklist

  1. Take 20+ Photos: Capture every ceiling spot, loose shingle pieces in the yard, and wide photos of every exterior roof slope.
  2. Cover Exposed Wood: Install heavy-duty plastic tarps over bare decking to stop secondary rain bands from ruining your ceiling drywall.
  3. Avoid Out-of-State Storm Chasers: Door-to-door sales crews flood Houston subdivisions after storms promising to pay your deductible. Waiving deductibles is illegal in Texas under House Bill 2102. Work only with established, local contractors with a verified physical Houston office.
  4. Book an In-Person Inspection: Have a HAAG-certified or GAF Master Elite local roofer inspect the roof pitch safely with safety harnesses to chalk and record every wind crease. If damage is isolated, patching isolated shingle sections may resolve the leak quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check my Houston roof for damage after a hurricane without climbing on it?

Stand in your yard with binoculars to check the ridge line for torn shingles, examine the perimeter ground for fallen roofing granules, and inspect metal downspouts for wind dents. Look for displaced pipe jacks and lifted flashing along exterior walls. In Texas windstorm zones, safety comes first: never step onto a wet, storm-damaged roof with compromised decking.

What should I do first if water is actively dripping through my ceiling after a tropical storm?

Puncture the center of the sagging ceiling drywall with a screwdriver to drain trapped water into a bucket, move valuables out of the room, and take clear timestamped photos of the leak. Call a licensed roofer immediately for emergency roof tarping to prevent mold and structural wood rot.

How much roof damage from a hurricane is needed for insurance to replace the entire roof in Texas?

Most Texas insurance policies follow the standard that if more than 30% of the roof surface has wind creases, torn shingles, or blown-off decking across multiple slopes, the carrier approves a full roof replacement rather than spot repairs.

Can I file an insurance claim if only my ridge caps blew off during the storm?

Yes. Blown-off hip and ridge caps expose the top decking gap directly to rainfall. You should file a claim if the cost to repair the ridge assembly, underlying membrane, and water intrusion exceeds your policy deductible.
