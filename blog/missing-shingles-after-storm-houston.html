Missing Shingles After a Storm: Emergency Repair Guide

Missing Shingles After a Storm: Emergency Repair Guide

By Josh, Lead Inspector

You wake up the morning after a Gulf storm and find shingles scattered across your lawn in Katy — a tab corner here, a full shingle there, and a bare patch staring back at you from the roof. That sinking feeling is justified. Every missing shingle is a gap in your home's armor, and Houston weather doesn't give you much time to think.

I've climbed hundreds of storm-hit roofs around Katy and Cypress, and the pattern is always the same: the damage looks small from the driveway and worse up close. This guide walks you through what missing shingles actually expose, what to do today, and how to decide between a tarp, a repair, or a full replacement.

What One Missing Shingle Actually Exposes

Shingles work as a team. Each row overlaps the one below it, shedding water down and off the roof. Pull one shingle out of that chain and you expose the underlayment beneath it — plus the nail heads that held everything together.

Underlayment is a backup layer, not a roof. Most Houston homes have synthetic underlayment or felt under the shingles, and both will hold off water for a short while. But "a short while" in Houston can mean one afternoon thunderstorm. Once water gets past the underlayment, it reaches your decking — the wood boards under everything. Wet decking swells, softens, and rots. That's when a simple shingle repair turns into decking replacement, and the bill climbs fast.

Wind rarely takes just one shingle cleanly, either. Around every missing shingle, expect lifted neighbors with broken seal strips and granule loss where debris scraped across the surface. Those lifted shingles are next in line to go.

Do These 3 Things Today

1. Photograph everything from the ground

Before anyone touches the roof, walk the perimeter with your phone. Capture the bare patches, the shingles in the yard, and any dents on gutters or AC units. Insurance adjusters decide based on what they can verify, and photos taken today are worth more than descriptions next week.

2. Check your attic

Grab a flashlight and look for daylight poking through the roof boards — that's a straight shot for rain. Feel the insulation under the damaged area; damp insulation means water is already in. Mark wet spots with tape so you can tell later whether they're spreading.

3. Get a tarp on it if rain is in the forecast

If the sky looks threatening, don't wait for a repair slot. A secured tarp over the damaged section keeps water out while you sort out the permanent fix. Our emergency roof tarping crew handles exactly this — fast, temporary protection that holds until repairs happen.

The honest rule I give homeowners: if your roof is under 10 years old and the damage is contained, repair it. If you're seeing missing shingles plus widespread lifting, curling, or granule loss, get a full assessment — patching a failing roof is money you'll spend twice.

What Insurance Actually Pays For

Here's the rule most Houston adjusters work from: if storm damage covers more than 30% of the roof surface, they typically approve a full replacement. Below that line, they pay to repair the damaged areas. Either way, most homeowners pay only their insurance deductible — not the whole bill.

One warning that matters in Texas: if anyone offers to "take care of" your deductible or waive it outright, walk away. Deductible waiving is illegal in Texas under House Bill 2102, and contractors who do it put your entire claim at risk. A straight contractor will talk openly about your deductible instead of pretending it doesn't exist. If you need a hand with the paperwork side, our help with your insurance claim walks you through documentation and adjuster meetings.

Can I wait a week to fix missing shingles after a storm?

I wouldn't. Exposed underlayment isn't built to face Houston sun and rain for long — UV breaks it down in days, and one hard thunderstorm can push water straight to your decking. What starts as three missing shingles becomes rotted wood and interior stains. If you can't get a repair scheduled this week, at least get the area tarped.

Will insurance pay for a full replacement if only a few shingles are missing?

Usually not for a full replacement — and that's normal. Most carriers approve a full replacement when storm damage covers more than 30% of the roof surface; below that, they pay to repair the damaged sections. Either way, most homeowners pay only their insurance deductible. Document everything with photos before any repairs, because adjusters decide based on what they can verify.

Three Mistakes That Make It Worse

Climbing up yourself on a wet roof. Storm-damaged roofs are slick, and lifted shingles hide soft spots. Every year people get hurt doing exactly this. Stay on the ladder, stay on the ground.

Letting a door-knocker start work that morning. After every big Houston storm, out-of-town crews flood neighborhoods like Cypress offering instant repairs. Get a local company with a real address — ours is 9402 Spaulding St, Houston, TX 77016 — and check reviews before anyone touches your roof.

Nailing new shingles over soft decking. If the wood underneath is spongy, new shingles just hide the rot for a season. A proper roof repair starts with solid decking, or you're paying twice.

Missing shingles feel like an emergency because they are one — but they're a solvable one. Tarp it if rain's coming, document everything, and get a straight assessment before the next storm rolls through. For broader wind and hail issues, see our storm damage repair guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many missing shingles mean I need a whole new roof?

There's no magic number — adjusters work off percentage, not shingle count. When storm damage covers more than 30% of the roof surface, most carriers approve a full replacement instead of patching. A roofer can measure the damaged area during an inspection. If the damage is scattered and the rest of your roof is sound, a proper repair is the right call.

My shingles are missing but nothing is leaking. Can I relax?

Not quite. No leak today doesn't mean no damage — water may be sitting in your underlayment or decking where you can't see it, and Houston humidity speeds up rot once wood stays damp. Check your attic for daylight through the boards or damp insulation. If the decking looks dry and no rain is coming, you have a little breathing room — but don't stretch it past a week or two.

Should I file an insurance claim for a few missing shingles?

Start with documentation, not a claim. Photograph the damage from the ground and in the attic, then get a roofer's read on the extent. Weigh the repair scope against your deductible — remember, most homeowners pay only their insurance deductible on approved claims. If the damage is clearly storm-related and beyond minor, filing makes sense. Never let a contractor promise to waive your deductible; that's illegal in Texas under House Bill 2102.

How long will an emergency tarp hold?

A properly installed tarp buys you days to a few weeks — it's a bandage, not a fix. Houston wind and UV punish tarps fast, so check it after every storm. Use that window to get a real repair scheduled. If your tarp is still up after a month, you're overdue.
