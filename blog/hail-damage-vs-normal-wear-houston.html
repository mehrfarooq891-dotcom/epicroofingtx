Hail Damage vs Normal Wear: How to Tell the Difference

By Josh, Lead Inspector • Published: August 19, 2026 • 10 Min Read • Hail Damage

More than 60 percent of initial roof insurance claim denials across Harris and Fort Bend counties happen because adjusters classify severe storm impact as ordinary maintenance wear and tear.

If your neighborhood in Cypress or Katy recently took a hit from hail, you are likely wondering whether those dark divots on your shingles represent real storm damage or just normal aging. Knowing the physical difference before your insurance adjuster climbs their ladder is the difference between a fully funded roof replacement and an out-of-pocket denial.

As a local roofer who evaluates storm damage every spring across Houston, I see how confusing shingle wear can be. Let us look closely at the direct physical differences between genuine hail strikes and common heat-related shingle aging.

Physical Characteristics: Hail Strikes vs. Thermal Aging

Asphalt shingles consist of a woven fiberglass backing mat saturated with asphalt and coated with ceramic mineral granules. Those granules serve one primary purpose: shielding the petroleum-based asphalt layer from brutal ultraviolet sun rays.

When hail strikes an asphalt shingle, the damage is violent, concentrated, and localized. When a roof ages in the Texas sun, the deterioration is uniform, widespread, and gradual.

Diagnostic Feature Genuine Hail Impact Normal Age & Heat Wear
Touch & Density Soft, spongy indentation when pressed with a thumb; fiberglass mat is bruised or fractured. Hard, brittle, firm base; surrounding asphalt does not compress under thumb pressure.
Pattern & Distribution Random, scattered marks across wind-facing slopes; impact points vary in size. Even, uniform granule thinning across all slopes, especially south and west faces.
Granule Loss Appearance Dark, circular divots with granules embedded deep into the asphalt or missing cleanly. Widespread horizontal bald patches exposing black asphalt or white fiberglass fibers.
Collateral Evidence Dents on metal ridge vents, lead pipe jacks, AC condenser fins, and metal gutters. No metal dents; metal accessories show only paint fading, surface rust, or light oxidation.
Cracking Pattern Circular or semi-circular fractures centered on the strike point. Straight horizontal or diagonal surface cracks caused by thermal expansion and contraction.

What does hail damage look like on shingles compared to normal blistering?

Hail damage creates soft, dark, random indentations where ice punched granules deep into the asphalt mat, leaving exposed fiberglass that feels spongy to touch. Normal heat blistering forms small, hard, brittle ring craters with raised edges caused by trapped gas expanding during 100-degree Houston summers without soft underlayment bruising.

How Blistering Happens in Houston Attics

The most common condition adjusters confuse with hail is asphalt blistering. In poorly ventilated Houston attics where temperatures soar past 140°F during July and August, moisture trapped inside the shingle during manufacturing vaporizes and expands.

This expanding vapor pushes upward against the granule surface, creating tiny asphalt blisters. When foot traffic or sun exposure pops these blisters, a small circular pit remains with a hollow raised rim.

Here is how HAAG-certified inspectors separate blisters from hail strikes:

  • Rim Examination: Blisters have sharp, raised, crater-like edges where the asphalt bubble popped open. Hail strikes have depressed, sunken edges driven downward into the roof substrate.
  • Attic Inspection: Blisters occur most heavily over unconditioned attic spaces with poor soffit ventilation. Hail strikes occur across exterior exposures regardless of attic airflow.
  • Soft Metal Check: If the shingles have hundreds of round pits but your aluminum box vents and gutter downspouts are smooth as glass, your roof has heat blistering, not hail damage.

The Chemistry of Thermal Shock and Shingle Aging

In Houston, roofs endure severe thermal shock cycles. A roof surface can reach 160°F during an August afternoon before a sudden gulf thunderstorm drops surface temperatures to 75°F in five minutes. This rapid contraction makes asphalt brittle over time.

As shingles age past 12 to 15 years, the asphalt binder loses its volatile oils. The granules naturally shed into the gutters during heavy rainstorms. This gradual loss leaves wide, flat bare zones rather than isolated, circular impact pits.

When hail hits an already aged shingle, it creates a fracture that goes completely through the dried-out mat. That is why age and storm impact often overlap on older roofs, making certified photographic documentation essential for claim approval.

The Insurance Industry Test Square Standard

When an insurance adjuster inspects your roof after a reported storm, they do not count every shingle on your house. They use a standardized chalk test square.

The adjuster measures a 10x10 foot area (100 square feet, or one roofing square) on each directional slope of your home. They rub sidewalk chalk sideways across the shingles to highlight indentations, circling every verifiable impact mark.

In Texas, the standard threshold for roof replacement approval is 10+ hail hits inside a 10x10 ft test square across multiple slopes. If your roof reaches this count on three or more directional facets, and more than 30% of the roof surface shows functional compromise, the adjuster generates an estimate for full replacement.

Why Minor Hail Strikes Still Need Professional Repair

Some homeowners walk outside after a storm, see that water is not pouring through their ceiling, and assume their roof is fine. That is a dangerous assumption in Southeast Texas.

Hail knocks granules loose, which shortens the roof's lifespan and gives water a path into the decking. Once direct sunlight reaches the exposed asphalt layer, UV rays dry out the volatile oils in 6 to 12 months. The shingle dries, curls, and cracks down to the plywood decking, causing widespread leaks long after the storm date has passed.

In Houston, standard shingles last 15 to 20 years, with premium GAF systems reaching 20 to 25 years. A severe hailstorm can instantly cut 8 to 10 years off that lifespan.

When damage is widespread, a full asphalt shingle replacement restores your home's structural shell. On a typical 2,000 square foot home, replacement costs $9,500 to $16,000, and with valid storm coverage, most homeowners pay only their insurance deductible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does hail damage look like on shingles compared to normal blistering?

Hail damage creates soft, dark, random indentations where ice punched granules deep into the asphalt mat, leaving exposed fiberglass that feels spongy to touch. Normal heat blistering forms small, hard, brittle ring craters with raised edges caused by trapped gas expanding during 100-degree Houston summers without soft underlayment bruising.

How many hail hits are required to get insurance to replace my roof in Houston?

Most Texas insurance adjusters look for at least 10+ hail hits inside a 10x10 ft test square across multiple roof slopes. If that density threshold is met and soft metals like gutters and AC fins show matching dents, carriers generally approve full replacement.

Can hail damage cause a leak months after the storm?

Yes. Hail fractures the asphalt mat and knocks loose protective granules. Over the next 6 to 12 months, Houston's intense heat and UV rays bake the exposed fiberglass, causing it to crack and let water seep into the decking during the next heavy rain.

How long do shingles last in Houston before normal wear requires replacement?

Standard asphalt shingles last 15 to 20 years in Houston due to extreme summer heat and humidity, while premium GAF architectural shingles reach 20 to 25 years maximum. Shingles older than 18 years showing widespread curling typically suffer from age wear rather than sudden storm impact.
