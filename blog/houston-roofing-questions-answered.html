Two things can slow a crew down, and a straight contractor tells you about both up front. The first is safety: nobody climbs a roof in lightning, in high wind, or on a wet slope that will not hold footing — the crew waits for a safe window and tells you so. What does not change is the order of work: emergencies go first, dispatch is immediate, and tarping happens the same day.

How Fast Can a Roof Be Tarped After Storm Damage?

A roof can be tarped the same day you call, as long as it is safe to climb: our emergency crews run 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year, with pre-loaded trucks ready for rapid dispatch. The tarp itself goes on in a single visit — a heavy UV-resistant membrane wrapped around wooden 2x4 battens and fastened into the rafters. If lightning, high wind, or a wet and unstable roof makes climbing unsafe, the crew waits for a safe window and tells you so — a tarp is never worth an injury.

Speed matters because Texas homeowners policies carry a duty to limit further damage after a loss: an open roof that keeps leaking while you wait can cost you the later water-damage part of a claim. A tarp installed the same day stops that clock — it seals the open decking and keeps rain out of the attic and ceilings, and a professionally installed tarp can safely stay in place for 30 to 90 days while the permanent repair is scoped and approved.

A proper emergency tarp is not a blue sheet held down with bricks. The membrane is wrapped around wooden battens and mechanically fastened into the rafters, which is why it holds through heavy Texas thunderstorm winds up to 60 mph instead of peeling off in the next gust. The crew also photographs the damage and the finished tarp setup, so you have a structured folder ready for your adjuster. Is a Roof Inspection Really Free — and What Does It Include?

Yes — completely free, with zero catch, zero pressure, and no obligation to buy anything. The inspection is a hands-on 21-point structural assessment, and you receive an annotated high-resolution photo report and a clear written estimate. You do not even have to be home, as long as the crew can reach the ladder location and the backyard.

The 21 points cover the whole system, not just the shingles you can see from the street: shingle integrity, hail granule loss, thermal seal adhesion, pipe boot flashings, valley metal, chimney counter-flashings, and moisture in the attic decking. Every finding is photographed, so the written report shows you the damage — and that photo record is exactly what an adjuster needs if the damage turns into a claim. Book it from our free roof inspection page, or read what a free inspection includes before you call.

Two things homeowners worry about are worth answering plainly. First, a free inspection does not trigger an insurance claim: the inspector documents the evidence first, and you decide whether to file only after reviewing the photos — so a small problem never becomes an unnecessary zero-dollar claim on your policy history. Second, free does not mean thin: a paid inspection elsewhere in Houston typically runs $150 to $450, and the free version here covers the same ground.

How Long Does a Roof Replacement Take in Houston?

Most standard asphalt shingle roofs in Houston are stripped, prepped, re-decked, and finished within 1 to 2 workdays. Detailed clay tile or custom standing seam layouts take 3 to 5 business days. Weather, decking damage found under the old roof, and permit or inspection timing can stretch either number — and a straight contractor tells you that before the first shingle comes off.

Size and complexity decide where your roof lands in that range. A straightforward single-story shingle roof is usually a one-day tear-off and a second day for install and cleanup; a larger two-story home, a steep pitch, or a roof cut up with valleys, skylights, and chimneys adds time. The work itself follows the same order every time: tear off the old roof, inspect and repair the decking underneath, lay underlayment and flashing, install the new system, and clean the property — including a nail sweep of the yard. See the full scope on our roof replacement in Houston page.

The honest variables are the ones no quote can fix in advance. Houston rain can pause a job mid-day, and rotted decking only shows itself once the old shingles are off — both are normal, and both should be explained to you the day they happen, not discovered on the final invoice. What happens before install day matters just as much: a free inspection, a written scope with decking terms per sheet, and materials delivered before the crew arrives are what keep a 1-to-2-day job a 1-to-2-day job.

What Should I Do First After a Storm Damages My Roof?

First, stay off the roof and photograph the damage from the ground — missing shingles, dents, fallen branches, and any water coming inside. Then call a roofer so the damage is documented with photos and a written report before anything is moved or repaired. Finally, get the open roof covered the same day with an emergency tarp, because your policy requires you to limit further damage and waiting can cost you the later water-damage part of a claim.

The order matters. Climbing a wet, steep, or storm-weakened roof is how homeowners get hurt after the storm has already passed — the photos a roofer needs can all be taken from the ground, and the close-ups happen during the inspection, with safety gear on. Do not start permanent repairs before the damage is documented either: once shingles are replaced, the evidence of what the storm did is gone.

Keep every receipt from the emergency work, including the tarping invoice — emergency mitigation costs are generally reimbursable with itemized receipts and damage photos. If the storm was hail, granule loss and soft dents are easy to miss from the ground and best documented within the first two days, so read the first 48 hours after hail damage and book a free inspection the same day you call.
