Public Adjuster vs Insurance Company Adjuster for Emergency Claims

By Josh, Lead Inspector • Published: October 10, 2026 • 10 Min Read • Insurance Claims
By Josh, Lead Inspector

Your insurance adjuster just left, and the number they quoted would not cover half your roof. A neighbor in Katy tells you to hire a public adjuster. Another neighbor in Cypress says that is a waste of money. Who is right? I have stood on roofs with both kinds of adjusters for years, and the honest answer is: it depends entirely on your claim.

Most Houston homeowners never need to know what a public adjuster is — until the morning their claim comes back denied or short by ten thousand dollars. Then the question gets urgent and expensive fast, because a public adjuster takes a cut of whatever they recover. Before you sign anything, you need to understand who each adjuster works for, what each one costs you, and the third option most people never hear about.

Who Each Adjuster Actually Works For

This is the whole game, so let me be blunt. The insurance company's adjuster — sometimes called a staff adjuster, sometimes an independent adjuster hired by the carrier — is paid by your insurance company. Their job is to evaluate your loss fairly under the policy terms, but their paycheck comes from the company writing the check. That does not make them villains; most are professionals. It does mean their incentives point one direction.

A public adjuster is licensed by the Texas Department of Insurance and hired by you. They manage your claim, argue your case, and negotiate the settlement — for a percentage of the payout, typically 10% in Texas, which is the state's cap on public adjuster fees. On a $20,000 roof claim, that is $2,000 out of your recovery. Worth it when they turn a denial into an approval. Painful when they add little to a claim that would have been approved anyway.

Look at that last column. Before either adjuster matters, someone has to prove the damage exists. Insurance approves roof replacement when storm damage covers more than 30% of the roof surface or when a proper test square shows 10+ hail hits inside a 10x10 ft area across multiple slopes. That proof comes from a roofer on the roof with chalk, a camera, and measurements — not from an adjuster behind a desk.

How much does a public adjuster charge in Texas?

Texas caps public adjuster fees at 10% of the insurance settlement — so on a $25,000 roof claim, the maximum fee is $2,500. The fee comes out of your recovery, not on top of it. Always get the fee agreement in writing before signing, and confirm the adjuster holds an active Texas Department of Insurance license, which you can verify on the TDI website.

When a Public Adjuster Earns Their Fee

Hire one when the math works: your claim was denied outright, the payout is thousands short of the real repair cost, or the damage is complex — like a Humble home with hail, wind, and pre-existing wear all mixed together and the carrier is blaming everything on age. A good public adjuster knows policy language, deadlines, and negotiation, and on a $30,000 dispute their 10% fee can be the best money you spend.

  • Claim denied: a public adjuster can demand reinspection with fresh evidence.
  • Lowballed payout: they negotiate line items the carrier minimized or missed.
  • Complex damage: they separate storm loss from wear so the carrier cannot blur them.
  • No time to fight: they handle the calls, letters, and follow-ups for you.

When You Do Not Need One

Here is what the public adjuster ads will not tell you: most clean storm claims in Houston get approved without one. When a hailstorm hits Cypress and your roof shows clear test-square damage across three slopes, the carrier's adjuster sees the same hail you do. What wins those claims is not negotiation — it is documentation. Dated photos, chalked test squares, collateral damage on gutters and AC fins, and a roofer present at the inspection to point at the evidence.

I have watched homeowners hand over 10% of a $22,000 settlement — $2,200 — on claims my documentation had already won before the public adjuster made a single call. If your damage is clear and your paperwork is solid, start with strong contractor-backed claim documentation and keep the public adjuster as your backup plan, not your first call.

Can I hire a public adjuster after my roof claim was denied?

Yes — after a denial is one of the best times to hire one. In Texas you have two years from the date of loss to dispute a claim, and a public adjuster can request a reinspection, submit supplemental evidence like new test-square documentation, and negotiate directly with the carrier. Act quickly: roofs weather further, evidence gets harder to prove, and missed deadlines can close the door for good.

Watch Out for the Deductible Trap

A warning that matters more than anything else in this article: if any contractor or adjuster promises to "handle" your deductible — waive it, absorb it, rebate it — walk away. Deductible waiving is illegal in Texas under House Bill 2102, and participating can void your claim or worse. On a legitimate approved claim, most homeowners pay only their insurance deductible. Anyone offering you a deal sweeter than that is not offering you a deal at all.

The smart sequence for most Houston roof claims: first, get thorough professional documentation of the damage. Second, file with that evidence in hand. Third, if the carrier denies or lowballs you, then bring in a public adjuster with the evidence file already built. You will pay the 10% only when there is real money being fought over. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check my Houston roof for damage after a hurricane without climbing on it?

Stand in your yard with binoculars to check the ridge line for torn shingles, examine the perimeter ground for fallen roofing granules, and inspect metal downspouts for wind dents. Look for displaced pipe jacks and lifted flashing along exterior walls. In Texas windstorm zones, safety comes first: never step onto a wet, storm-damaged roof with compromised decking.

What should I do first if water is actively dripping through my ceiling after a tropical storm?

Puncture the center of the sagging ceiling drywall with a screwdriver to drain trapped water into a bucket, move valuables out of the room, and take clear timestamped photos of the leak. Call a licensed roofer immediately for emergency roof tarping to prevent mold and structural wood rot.

How much roof damage from a hurricane is needed for insurance to replace the entire roof in Texas?

Most Texas insurance policies follow the standard that if more than 30% of the roof surface has wind creases, torn shingles, or blown-off decking across multiple slopes, the carrier approves a full roof replacement rather than spot repairs.

Can I file an insurance claim if only my ridge caps blew off during the storm?

Yes. Blown-off hip and ridge caps expose the top decking gap directly to rainfall. You should file a claim if the cost to repair the ridge assembly, underlying membrane, and water intrusion exceeds your policy deductible.
