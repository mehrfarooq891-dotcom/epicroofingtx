What Does a Roof Actually Cost in Houston?
An Honest Pricing Guide

Two quotes for the same Houston roof can land thousands of dollars apart. Here is what a roof price is actually made of — and how to tell an honest quote from a cheap one.

📅 November 6, 2026 ⏱️ 11 Min Read 👤 Epic Roofing TX Team 📍 Houston & Fort Bend County, TX

Direct Answer: What Does a Roof Cost in Houston?

In 2026, a standard roof replacement in Houston ranges from $9,000 to $18,000. Standard 3-tab shingles run $6,500 to $10,500, architectural shingles $9,500 to $16,500, and standing seam metal $18,500 to $35,000. Five things set your final number: roof size and pitch, material, tear-off and decking, ventilation and flashing, and warranty level. A free inspection with written, line-item pricing tells you where your roof lands.

“Affordable” is the most abused word in Houston roofing. Every company uses it, and no two mean the same thing by it. One homeowner gets three quotes for the same roof and the numbers land thousands of dollars apart — and every salesperson insists theirs is the affordable one. A roof price is a short list of measurable things: how big your roof is, what it is made of, and what is hiding underneath the old shingles. This guide works through each one using the price ranges we publish on our own cost pages, so you can read any quote — including ours — and know what you are paying for. For the short version first, see our guide to affordable roofing in Houston.

What “Affordable” Should Mean — and the Cheap-Roof Traps

A fair roof price is one you can check: every line names a real material or a real task, the total matches the scope of work, and the company is still in Houston when you need them next year. The cheapest bid usually looks cheap because something real was left out of it — and the missing piece shows up later as a leak, a repair bill, or a second roof far sooner than it should be.

Red Flags: Storm-Chaser Pricing and No-Address Contractors

After every big hail or wind event in Houston, out-of-town crews appear at front doors with prices that expire today and paperwork that names no local address. A real Houston company has a Houston address you can drive past and does not need a same-day signature. Watch for the rest of the pattern too: one lump-sum number with no line items, a large cash payment demanded before materials arrive, and any offer to absorb or waive your insurance deductible — illegal in Texas under House Bill 2102, and a company advertising it is telling you it cuts corners where you cannot see them. For the full warning list, read our guide to storm chaser roofers before you let anyone on your roof.

The True Cost of the Cheapest Bid — Rework, Voided Manufacturer Warranties, Leaks in Year Two

The cheapest bid rarely stays the cheapest. The usual savings come from skipping the tear-off and nailing new shingles over old ones, reusing flashing that should have been replaced, or leaving the attic ventilation as it was. A second layer hides rotted decking instead of fixing it, and that decking keeps rotting under the new roof. Reused flashing is the first place a Houston roof leaks, usually in the second year, right after the cheap crew has moved on to the next storm town. And a manufacturer warranty is only as good as the installation behind it — if the install does not follow the manufacturer’s requirements, the coverage may not be there when you need it. A quote thousands below the others is a list of things someone decided not to do.

Once you know them, you can read any estimate the way a roofer reads it — and spot the one that left an ingredient out. 1 — Roof Size, Pitch and Complexity

Size is the biggest driver, and roofers measure it in squares — one square equals 100 square feet of roof surface. A 2,000-square-foot home typically carries 22 to 25 squares, and the table above shows how the price climbs with it. Pitch matters too: a steep roof needs safety rigging and slower, more careful work, and our cost guide puts that extra labor at 15% to 30% above a walkable slope. Complexity adds the rest — every valley, dormer, chimney, and skylight is a place where water changes direction, and each one takes custom flashing and extra time.

2 — Material: 3-Tab vs Architectural Shingles vs Metal

Material is the second driver. By the square, standard 3-tab asphalt runs $350 to $450, architectural shingles $500 to $650, standing seam metal $950 to $1,450, and concrete and synthetic slate $1,200 to $1,800. That is why the same house can be quoted at $6,500 to $10,500 in 3-tab and $18,500 to $35,000 in standing seam metal. Lifespan moves with the price: 3-tab shingles typically last 12 to 15 years in Houston, architectural shingles 20 to 25+ years, standing seam metal 45 to 50+ years, and concrete and synthetic slate 50+ years. Wind resistance follows the same ladder — 3-tab up to 60 MPH winds, architectural up to 110/130 MPH, standing seam up to 140+ MPH, and concrete and synthetic slate up to 130+ MPH.

3 — Tear-Off Layers and Decking Condition

Texas law allows a maximum of two layers of shingles before a full tear-off down to the bare wood is required, so a roof already carrying two layers costs more to strip — more labor, more disposal, more time. Then there is what the tear-off reveals: standard estimates assume your deck boards are in good condition, but hidden leaks rot plywood and pine decking from the inside, and rotted boards cannot hold nails. Replacing damaged decking typically costs $75 to $110 per sheet of wood. Nobody can count the bad sheets before the old roof comes off, which is why an honest quote states the per-sheet price in writing instead of pretending the number is zero. If a quote has no decking line, ask what happens when the crew finds soft wood.

4 — Ventilation, Flashing and Code Upgrades

Attic ventilation keeps Houston’s heat and humidity from cooking shingles from below and trapping moisture in the decking — new shingles on a broken ventilation setup is a new roof with an old problem. Flashing is the same story at every joint: dormers, chimneys, skylights, valleys, and wall junctions all need precise custom flashing and sealing, because those are the places water finds first. Then there are the code upgrades: homes in designated coastal and windstorm zones may need a Texas Department of Insurance windstorm inspection (WPI-8) for insurance eligibility, and that inspection has to be built into the job, not discovered after the roof is on. None of this shows in a photograph of a finished roof — which is why cheap quotes leave it out.

5 — Warranty Level and Workmanship Coverage

There are two coverages on every job, and they are not the same thing: the manufacturer’s coverage for the material itself, and the installer’s coverage for the workmanship — how the roof was actually put together. A quote that names neither is asking you to trust a handshake. Ask for both in writing, with the documents named, before you sign: what the manufacturer requires from the installation for its coverage to hold, and who pays if a workmanship problem shows up in the first few years. For how the two coverages differ and what to demand in writing, read our guide to roof warranties explained.

Repair vs Replacement: Where the Money Really Goes

The honest answer starts with what is actually wrong. A repair fixes a defined problem — a failed flashing detail, a small section of wind-lifted shingles, a single leak path an inspection can trace to one spot. The work is local, the materials are few, and the rest of the roof keeps serving. A replacement is a different decision: the whole system has reached the end of its service life, the damage is spread across multiple slopes, or the roof has already been repaired enough times that the next repair is just the next patch on a tired system. That is where the $9,000 to $18,000 range in this guide comes in — a full replacement renews the shingles, the underlayment, the flashing, and the worn-out details in one job, instead of paying for them one leak at a time. Frequently Asked Questions About Roof Costs in Houston

What does a roof replacement cost in Houston in 2026?

A standard roof replacement in Houston ranges from $9,000 to $18,000 in 2026. Standard 3-tab shingles run $6,500 to $10,500, architectural shingles $9,500 to $16,500, and standing seam metal $18,500 to $35,000. Size, pitch, material, and decking decide where your roof lands.

How much does a roof cost per square in Houston?

By the square — one square equals 100 square feet of roof surface — standard 3-tab asphalt costs $350 to $450, architectural shingles $500 to $650, standing seam metal $950 to $1,450, and concrete and synthetic slate $1,200 to $1,800.

What makes one Houston roof quote higher than another?

Five things: roof size and pitch, material, tear-off layers and decking condition, ventilation and flashing work, and warranty level. A steep, complex roof with two old layers and soft decking costs more than a simple single-layer roof of the same size. Compare the line items, not just the totals.

Is it cheaper to repair my roof or replace it?

A repair is cheaper when the problem is local — one flashing detail, one leak path, one damaged section — and the rest of the roof still has service life left. A replacement is the better spend when the damage is spread across the roof or the system is at the end of its life, because repeated repairs on a worn-out roof add up to replacement money without a new roof at the end of it.
