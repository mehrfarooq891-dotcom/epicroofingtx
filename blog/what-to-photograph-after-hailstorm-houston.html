What to Photograph After a Hailstorm for Your Insurance File

By Josh, Lead Inspector • Published: August 19, 2026 • 10 Min Read • Insurance Claims

Your smartphone camera roll is the single most valuable tool you own the morning after severe hail sweeps across Southeast Texas.

When you submit an insurance claim for storm damage, your adjuster is not taking your word for it. They work strictly off photographic proof. If your photos are blurry, lack scale, or fail to document collateral impact around your yard, your claim can be stalled for weeks or denied completely as pre-existing wear.

I have helped hundreds of property owners across Houston, Humble, and Pearland document insurance files that get approved on the very first review. Here is your step-by-step photographic checklist to build a solid insurance file before the adjuster arrives.

1. Immediate Ground Photos (Before the Ice Melts)

If hail fell recently, speed is critical. The insurance carrier will verify local National Weather Service radar logs, but having clear proof of hail diameter on your specific driveway removes all doubt.

  • Hail Measurement Photo: Pick up 3 to 5 representative hail stones from your lawn or patio. Place them beside a ruler, a tape measure, or a coin (a quarter measures exactly 0.955 inches). Take crisp, well-lit photos from directly above.
  • Hail Accumulation Shot: Take a wide photo of your driveway, patio, or lawn showing hail coverage depth before the warm Texas air dissolves the ice.
  • Freezer Storage: Bag a few stones in a freezer bag and store them. Adjusters appreciate seeing actual ice samples during their inspection visit.

What photos do insurance companies require for a Texas roof hail claim?

Insurance carriers require wide-angle shots of every roof slope, close-ups of soft metal collateral damage on AC fins and gutters with a coin for scale, and chalked 10x10 ft test squares showing 10+ distinct hail hits. Always enable date-and-time stamps in your camera settings to prove storm date correlation.

2. Collateral Damage Photos (The Adjuster's Deciding Factor)

Adjusters inspect soft metals first because aluminum and copper retain permanent impact dents that cannot be faked or confused with normal aging. If soft metals on your property are dented, adjusters are far more likely to agree that the shingles are compromised.

Walk your property perimeter and capture high-resolution photos of these specific components:

  • AC Condenser Unit: Photograph the thin aluminum radiator fins on your outside HVAC unit. Hail impacts bend these fins flat, restricting airflow and proving storm direction.
  • Metal Gutters and Downspouts: Take angled photos along the gutter runs. Hail strikes leave distinct rounded dimples.
  • Window Screens and Frames: Check vinyl window frames for cracks and metal screens for punctures or mesh tears.
  • Mailbox, Decking & Wood Fences: Fresh hail impacts strip oxidation off wooden fence pickets, leaving bright, clean circular spots known as hail spatter.
  • Lead and Aluminum Pipe Jacks: Photograph the metal vent pipes on the lower edge of your roof line showing circular strike marks.

3. Roof Surface Documentation: What a Pro Should Capture

Do not climb on your roof. Steep pitches and loose granules after a hailstorm make roofs extremely slick. Have a licensed, HAAG-certified roofer capture the necessary high-angle roof photos safely.

Your roofer should photograph:

  • Wide Slope Overviews: Broad shots showing the full plane of every slope (Front, Rear, Left, Right).
  • 10x10 Chalk Test Squares: The roofer should chalk a 100-square-foot square on each slope, circling every strike. In Texas, the benchmark is 10+ hail hits inside a 10x10 ft test square across multiple slopes.
  • Macro Close-Ups with Thumb Pressure: Close shots showing exposed black asphalt, fractured fiberglass scrim, and dislodged ceramic granules.
  • Ridge and Hip Caps: Detailed shots of cracked cap shingles along roof peaks.
  • Flashing and Valley Channels: Close-up photos of metal valley liners and step flashing showing impact craters.

4. Interior Leak and Water Damage Photos

If the storm produced puncturing hail or cracked flashings, inspect your living areas immediately:

  • Take wide room shots showing ceiling water stains relative to exterior walls.
  • Take close-up photos of bubbling paint, peeling drywall tape, or warped baseboards.
  • Photograph the attic decking directly beneath the roof leak to show water tracks leading from the shingle penetration down to your ceiling framing.
  • Document insulation saturation levels using a flashlight to show darkened, compressed fiberglass batts.

If water is actively leaking inside, arrange for securing tarp protection over active punctures immediately to keep your policy valid under your homeowner mitigation obligations.

5. Metadata, File Naming, and Submission Best Practices

Insurance adjusters process dozens of files each week. If your documentation is messy, your claim sits at the bottom of the pile. When you transfer photos from your phone, keep the EXIF metadata intact so the exact GPS coordinates and capture timestamps remain embedded in the raw file.

When you upload photos to your carrier portal, organization matters. Name your image files logically (for example, 01-AC-Unit-Hail-Dents.jpg, 02-Front-Slope-Test-Square.jpg, 03-Rear-Gutter-Dimples.jpg). Include a short itemized summary sheet with dates and brief captions.

Under Texas insurance standards, if storm damage affects more than 30% of the roof surface, carriers typically approve a complete roof replacement rather than patchy repairs. For a 2,000 sq ft Houston home, replacement runs $9,500 to $16,000, and with approved RCV coverage, most homeowners pay only their insurance deductible.

6. Common Photo Mistakes That Delay Claim Approval

Avoid these three frequent mistakes when sending photos to your insurer:

  • Using Heavy Digital Zoom: Digital zoom pixelates shingle grain. Step closer with a safe ladder setup or have a professional take high-resolution optical close-ups.
  • Taking Photos in Direct Noon Sun: Midday glare washes out soft metal indentations. Early morning or late afternoon sun creates natural shadows that make hail dimples stand out clearly.
  • Omitting Wider Yard Context: If you only photograph five close-up shingle hits without showing the full slope, the adjuster may assume the hits were isolated or staged. Always pair wide contextual shots with tight close-ups.

Frequently Asked Questions

What photos do insurance companies require for a Texas roof hail claim?

Insurance carriers require wide-angle shots of every roof slope, close-ups of soft metal collateral damage on AC fins and gutters with a coin for scale, and chalked 10x10 ft test squares showing 10+ distinct hail hits. Always enable date-and-time stamps in your camera settings to prove storm date correlation.

How should I photograph hail stones before they melt?

Place hail stones immediately next to a tape measure, quarter, or golf ball on a hard surface, take high-resolution photos showing the measurement clearly, and place a few samples in a zip-top bag inside your freezer for your adjuster to inspect.

Should I climb on my roof to photograph hail damage myself?

No. Walking on a damaged roof can void shingle warranties and cause severe falls. Take ground-level collateral photos of siding, gutters, window screens, and air conditioning units, then hire a licensed, insured roofer to photograph the high-slope shingles safely.

Why are photos of my air conditioner and gutters important for a roof claim?

Insurance adjusters use soft metal components like aluminum gutters and thin AC condenser fins as baseline collateral evidence. Dents on soft metals prove the storm produced ice large and dense enough to fracture asphalt shingles on the same property.
