Storm Damage Insurance Claim Timeline:
What Happens After You File in Houston

You've submitted your initial loss notice after a violent hailstorm or severe weather front rolled through Harris County. Here is the exact chronological timeline of what happens next—from adjuster visits to payout release.

📅 Published: August 9, 2026 ⏱️ 11 Min Read 🏷️ Category: Insurance Claims

You have pressed submit on your insurance provider's online portal or hung up the phone after notifying your agent that severe weather battered your property. Your claim number is in hand, but now you enter a phase that causes intense anxiety for many Houston homeowners: waiting for the machine of insurance adjustment to move forward.

Whether your home endured quarter-sized hail in Katy, gale-force straight-line winds in Spring, or torrential rain in Cypress, the post-filing timeline operates on specific contractual milestones and Texas statutory mandates. Understanding what happens behind the scenes during every week of the process allows you to prepare documentation, schedule contractor oversight, and avoid financial surprises.

Phase 1: Initial Claim Acknowledgment & Adjuster Assignment (Days 1 to 3)

Immediately after filing, your insurance company assigns a desk adjuster to manage your file administratively and dispatches a field adjuster (or an independent contracted adjuster) to perform the physical roof assessment.

Under the Texas Prompt Payment of Claims Act (Texas Insurance Code § 542), property insurance carriers operating in Texas are bound by strict statutory deadlines:

Texas Statutory Claim Deadlines

  • 15 Business Days: The insurer must acknowledge receipt of your claim, begin an investigation, and request all necessary documentation from you.
  • 15 Business Days Post-Documentation: Once the carrier receives all requested proof-of-loss reports, they must formally accept or reject your claim in writing.
  • 5 Business Days: After agreeing to pay all or part of your claim, the insurer must issue your settlement check.

In practice across Greater Houston, most major carriers assign a field adjuster within 24 to 72 hours of filing. You will receive a phone call or text message to schedule an on-site property inspection date, usually set for 3 to 7 days out depending on overall regional storm volume.

Phase 2: The On-Site Adjuster Inspection (Days 4 to 10)

The physical inspection is the single most critical juncture in your insurance claim timeline. The field adjuster visits your property to measure roof square footage, evaluate shingle conditions, count hail strike test squares (typically 10ft x 10ft sections on each roof slope), and inspect collateral soft metals like gutters, roof vents, and AC condenser fins.

During catastrophic storm surges across Harris and Fort Bend counties, field adjusters often manage 6 to 8 property inspections per day. Because of this high volume, an adjuster may spend as little as 20 to 30 minutes on your roof. This creates a significant risk that subtle or hidden damage—such as fractured fiberglass mats beneath architectural asphalt shingles or micro-creasing on metal seams—will be overlooked.

To prevent under-scoping, smart homeowners schedule a professional storm damage evaluation with a licensed local roofing contractor to be present during the adjuster's walk-through. Having a contractor on the roof alongside the adjuster ensures all damage is marked in chalk, documented with high-resolution photography, and agreed upon before the adjuster leaves the driveway.

Phase 3: Scope of Loss Summary & First Payout Check (Days 10 to 20)

Approximately 7 to 10 days after the physical inspection, your insurance company issues an official Explanation of Benefits (EOB) accompanied by a detailed Xactimate Scope of Loss estimate.

This document outlines line-item line costs for labor, tear-off, dump fees, underlayment, and replacement materials. It also establishes three crucial dollar amounts:

Financial Term Definition & How It Is Calculated When Funds Are Disbursed
RCV (Replacement Cost Value) The total estimated cost to replace your damaged roof today at prevailing local Houston contractor rates. Total claim ceiling (paid in two separate installments).
Deductible Your non-negotiable out-of-pocket obligation mandated by Texas House Bill 2102. Typically 1% to 2% of total insured dwelling value. Paid directly by you to your chosen roofer upon job completion.
ACV (Actual Cash Value) The current depreciated value of your old roof (RCV minus age depreciation minus your deductible). Issued as the First Check within 5 days of claim approval.
Recoverable Depreciation The remaining claim funds temporarily withheld by the insurer until restoration is completed. Issued as the Second Check after final contractor invoice submission.

Navigating these financial breakdowns is a foundational part of Texas insurance claim processes. Receiving your initial ACV check provides the capital needed to order materials and lock in your installation date.

Phase 4: Scope Reconciliation, Supplements & Mortgage Endorsements (Days 20 to 35)

Rarely does an initial insurance estimate match the exact real-world cost of a code-compliant roof replacement in Houston. Initial estimates frequently omit mandatory local municipal building codes, such as:

  • City of Houston drip edge flashing requirements
  • Ice and water shield membrane along eaves and valleys
  • Synthetic underlayment upgrades mandated by windstorm code
  • Proper attic ventilation ratios (soffit and ridge vent balancing)

During this phase, your contractor prepares and submits an itemized Supplement Request with photo evidence and code documentation directly to your desk adjuster. Reconciling supplements typically takes 5 to 10 business days.

Navigating Mortgage Company Endorsements

If you have a mortgage on your home, your initial ACV check will likely be made out jointly to both you and your mortgage company (e.g., Wells Fargo, Chase, Freedom Mortgage). Because the lender holds a lien on the property, they require a formal loss draft review before releasing funds.

You must endorse the check, complete a mortgage loss draft packet (including contractor W-9, signed contract, and scope of loss), and mail it to your lender's insurance department. This endorsement process typically adds 7 to 14 days to your timeline.

Phase 5: Roof Installation & Quality Verification (Days 30 to 45)

With scope approval finalized and initial funds cleared, your contractor schedules material delivery and crew deployment. For standard residential homes in Houston (2,000 to 3,500 sq ft), complete tear-off and replacement takes just 1 to 2 days.

If severe weather caused severe structural puncturing or ceiling leaks while waiting for claim clearance, your roofer should have already performed emergency roof tarping protection to prevent interior mold growth and structural timber rot.

Phase 6: Final Invoicing & Depreciation Release (Days 45 to 60)

Once your new roof is fully installed and passes final municipal inspection, your contractor sends a final Certificate of Completion along with an itemized final invoice to your insurance desk adjuster.

Within 5 to 10 business days of receiving the final completion proof, your insurance carrier releases the withheld Recoverable Depreciation check. You endorse this final check over to your contractor, pay your agreed-upon policy deductible, and receive your final warranty documentation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an insurance company have to pay a roof claim in Texas?

Under Texas Insurance Code § 542, insurers must acknowledge your claim within 15 business days, make an approval determination within 15 business days of receiving all proof documents, and issue payment within 5 business days of approval.

Why is my mortgage company listed on my insurance settlement check?

Your mortgage lender has a legal financial stake in your home. Insurers issue joint checks on property damage over $10,000 to ensure the settlement money is used to fully repair the structural asset rather than spent elsewhere.

What happens if the initial insurance payout check is not enough to cover the repair quote?

Your contractor will file an itemized supplement containing photographic proof, pitch measurements, and local Houston building code requirements. The insurance carrier reviews the supplement and adjusts the total RCV settlement upward to cover the shortfall.

Can I start temporary roof leak repairs before the adjuster visits my Houston home?

Yes. Property owners have a contractual responsibility under Texas insurance policies to mitigate ongoing damage. Installing temporary emergency tarps or stopping active leaks is expected, provided you photograph the damage beforehand and keep all itemized receipts.
